  • The gate now checks that a scene is visible, not merely rendered. All three critics independently reported scenes as blank while the gate called them rendered — because “renders at least once” only proves a shader compiled. Every scene is now sampled from the real frame buffer and fails if it has nothing in it (black), no structure (a flat wash), or is blown out. This is a strengthening of the gate, not a relaxation: no existing assertion was removed or loosened.
  • Building that check surfaced four bugs in itself before it was trustworthy — it sampled an intermediate post-processing target instead of the screen, then a 182×102 patch of the bottom-left corner (the darkest part of frame, because the grade vignettes corners), then stalled the GPU reading whole frames, then missed small bright features on too coarse a grid. The first two made it report black for scenes that screenshot at mean luma 81.
  • The one timing change: the scene-traversal test's wall clock was raised, because it walks twelve scene builds on a software renderer. The 60-second softlock guarantee is asserted per scene inside that test and is untouched.
  • Frame time is measured but not judged — this container renders through SwiftShader and software frame times are not real performance (CLAUDE.md § Gotchas).

Limitations of this round — read before trusting the table above

  • No reference footage was compared against. Neither this builder nor any critic can watch video. Every critic judged our frames against a written rubric derived from its bar, not side by side with the bar itself. Where a verdict says a stranger would pick the other one, that is a judgement from criteria, not from a comparison.
  • The controls/camera/polish bar was never played. Loading it needs Chromium to trust this container's proxy CA; it does not, and the only override is disabling TLS verification, which this environment forbids. So controls, camera feel and polish have not been judged against anything.
  • Stills cannot judge pacing, motion or audio. Every critic said so explicitly. The vignette runs about three minutes and was reviewed from five frames.

What changed this pass

  • Twelve scenes built, one complete path: content notes, vignette select, the heart attack, pronounced dead, the buzzing, the out-of-body lift, the tunnel, the ones who came, the Being of Light, the border, the choice, the life review. The harness scene is gone.
  • Systems: a beat director so pacing is authored as data; procedural audio with no asset files (a heartbeat that falters, a ring that rises, a drone that opens into the Light); a two-pass post pipeline whose grade does drain, aberration, distortion, vignette, grain, smear and tonemap in one shader; a damped camera rig with embodied and drifting modes.
  • Three systemic rendering bugs found and fixed: the glow form computed radial falloff on a sphere shell, where every fragment sits at the same radius, so it rendered as a flat disc; motes were square because PointsMaterial draws squares without a sprite; and the air shell, being a transparent sphere at the origin, was sorted after everything further away and painted whole scenes out. That last one is why several scenes were black.
  • The tunnel was rotated so its lit end was behind the camera, and its wall shader seamed down one line because it drove frequencies from a UV coordinate that wraps. Both fixed.
  • The Light was a white rectangle. Scaling a glow sprite until the camera is inside it fills the frame with one flat value. Replaced with a radiance shell whose filaments keep structure at any brightness.

Known, named, not yet fixed

  • The kitchen has too few objects to read as lived-in, and the death still changes the lens more than it changes the room. No prop in it does anything across the sequence.
  • Nothing in the Threshold has a horizon or an object of known size, so none of it conveys real scale.
  • The presences are additive glow with no boundary, no terminator and no occlusion, so they read as light rather than as bodies.
  • Front-matter typography is the browser's default UI stack, and the copy still talks about what is “built” and “planned” — release notes inside the game.
  • Captions are carrying beats the images do not, which is the opposite of the intent.

Two things the gate caught on its first run

Recorded because CLAUDE.md § Gotchas says to add to it every time a mistake is made or narrowly avoided. Both are now in that section.

  • Playwright / Chromium version skew. A caret range resolved Playwright to a version whose required Chromium build was not the one installed, and all 24 tests failed on a missing executable rather than on anything about the game. Fixed by pinning the Playwright version exactly, since that version determines the browser build.
  • A WebGL warning that was not ours. The gate failed on GPU stall due to ReadPixels. Before filtering it, it was tested against a page containing nothing but a canvas and gl.clear — no three.js, none of our code — which emitted the identical message. It is the headless compositor reading the framebuffer back, so it is filtered, scoped narrowly to that one driver performance message. Every other WebGL warning still fails the gate.
