  • The gate now checks that a scene is visible, not merely rendered. All three critics independently reported scenes as blank while the gate called them rendered — because “renders at least once” only proves a shader compiled. Every scene is now sampled from the real frame buffer and fails if it has nothing in it (black), no structure (a flat wash), or is blown out. This is a strengthening of the gate, not a relaxation: no existing assertion was removed or loosened.
  • Building that check surfaced four bugs in itself before it was trustworthy — it sampled an intermediate post-processing target instead of the screen, then a 182×102 patch of the bottom-left corner (the darkest part of frame, because the grade vignettes corners), then stalled the GPU reading whole frames, then missed small bright features on too coarse a grid. The first two made it report black for scenes that screenshot at mean luma 81.
  • The one timing change: the scene-traversal test's wall clock was raised, because it walks twelve scene builds on a software renderer. The 60-second softlock guarantee is asserted per scene inside that test and is untouched.
  • Frame time is measured but not judged — this container renders through SwiftShader and software frame times are not real performance (CLAUDE.md § Gotchas).

Limitations of this round — read before trusting the table above

  • No reference footage was compared against. Neither this builder nor any critic can watch video. Every critic judged our frames against a written rubric derived from its bar, not side by side with the bar itself. Where a verdict says a stranger would pick the other one, that is a judgement from criteria, not from a comparison.
  • The controls/camera/polish bar was never played. Loading it needs Chromium to trust this container's proxy CA; it does not, and the only override is disabling TLS verification, which this environment forbids. So controls, camera feel and polish have not been judged against anything.
  • Stills cannot judge pacing, motion or audio. Every critic said so explicitly. The vignette runs about three minutes and was reviewed from five frames.

What changed this pass

Scene coverage 12/39 → 21/39. Path A now runs life review → Council → seven Life Market aisles → checkout.

  • Pacing. The heart-attack vignette reached the afterlife at 3.5 minutes and then held its closing image indefinitely, so a player who simply watched could sit in a dead room well past that. It now runs 164s of beats plus a 10-second grace, after which the scene lets go by itself — 175s, under three minutes, with the exit live from the first frame for anyone who wants to leave sooner. That figure was only true at full frame rate. Trimming the beats fixed the script but not the clock: the timeline was advanced by the game loop’s clamped animation delta, so at roughly 3fps each 0.33s frame moved the story only 0.1s and the 165s vignette took over 500s to play. Pacing was a property of the machine, not of the game, which is why tuning beats could never fix it. The Director now advances to absolute wall-clock time since scene load, still bounded so a hidden tab does not skip beats nobody saw. The grace period had the identical bug — counting deltas to reach 10 seconds meant it took 27. 2:55, now on any renderer. Every beat was trimmed rather than any beat cut, so the arc keeps its shape. GAME_BRIEF.md was updated to match rather than left contradicting the build.
  • The Council. The guides weigh the heart against the feather, and the beam tilts by the karma the review recorded. Its real job is that it makes the ledger run end to end: the review writes karma, the Council reads it and locks obligations into the cart, and the Market honours those locks. Before this, karma was written and nothing read it.
  • The Life Market. Seven aisles and a checkout, sharing one mechanism rather than being seven hand-written scenes. 35 items, each carrying a sensory flash rather than a description. Locked obligations show as “already in your cart” and cannot be put back, which is what the brief specifies for unresolved karma.
  • The seven “Life Market aisle through checkout” journeys now pass. They had skipped on every run since the first commit. This is the first time that part of the brief has been exercised at all.

Two economy decisions worth a second opinion

  • The numbers are a first guess. All pricing lives in one table in src/game/systems/market.ts, deliberately, because an economy spread across eight scene files cannot be tuned. Nobody has played it. With the slice's single review writing −1 karma, a soul arrives with one locked lesson and very little to spend — which may be tight, or may just be stingy. Only playing it will say.
  • The gift flashes are written to be tempting. The brief allows a cart of only gifts and has the guides warn that such a life teaches little. That warning is only worth anything if the gifted life is genuinely attractive; written as obviously hollow, the choice would be theatre and the warning redundant.

Known, named, not yet fixed

  • The reincarnation loop is still open. The River of Forgetting and rebirth are unbuilt, so the cart never becomes the next run's opening conditions — the one mechanic the game is named for. Checkout names them rather than pretending the path continues.
  • Six of seven vignettes are unbuilt, including the two carrying the brief's hardest content rules. Path B does not exist at all.
  • The kitchen has too few objects to read as lived-in, and its death still changes the lens more than the room — the vignette critic's biggest gap, only partly addressed.
  • Nothing in the Threshold has a horizon or an object of known size, so none of it conveys real scale.
  • The presences have no boundary, terminator or occlusion, so they read as light rather than as bodies.
  • Front-matter typography is still the browser's default UI stack.
  • No critic has reviewed the Council or the Market. Every verdict on this page predates both.
