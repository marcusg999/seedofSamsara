  • The DMT pass has not re-run the full gate. It was built while a gate run held port 4173, and a second run would have collided with it. What was verified for the three new scenes, on a separate port against a throwaway dev server: typecheck, lint and production build clean; every GLSL program compiled and drew (zero entries in the renderer’s own onShaderError log, zero trapped page errors, zero console output beyond the known host ReadPixels notice); all five new exits land where they are declared to; every scene sampled from the real frame buffer well inside the visibility bounds; and no overlay left in the DOM after unload. The numbers are in “What changed this pass”. The full gate still has to be run before this is called passing.
  • The gate now checks that a scene is visible, not merely rendered. All three critics independently reported scenes as blank while the gate called them rendered — because “renders at least once” only proves a shader compiled. Every scene is now sampled from the real frame buffer and fails if it has nothing in it (black), no structure (a flat wash), or is blown out. This is a strengthening of the gate, not a relaxation: no existing assertion was removed or loosened.
  • Building that check surfaced four bugs in itself before it was trustworthy — it sampled an intermediate post-processing target instead of the screen, then a 182×102 patch of the bottom-left corner (the darkest part of frame, because the grade vignettes corners), then stalled the GPU reading whole frames, then missed small bright features on too coarse a grid. The first two made it report black for scenes that screenshot at mean luma 81.
  • The one timing change: the scene-traversal test's wall clock was raised, because it walks twelve scene builds on a software renderer. The 60-second softlock guarantee is asserted per scene inside that test and is untouched.
  • Frame time is measured but not judged — this container renders through SwiftShader and software frame times are not real performance (CLAUDE.md § Gotchas).

Limitations of this round — read before trusting the table above

  • No reference footage was compared against. Neither this builder nor any critic can watch video. Every critic judged our frames against a written rubric derived from its bar, not side by side with the bar itself. Where a verdict says a stranger would pick the other one, that is a judgement from criteria, not from a comparison.
  • The controls/camera/polish bar was never played. Loading it needs Chromium to trust this container's proxy CA; it does not, and the only override is disabling TLS verification, which this environment forbids. So controls, camera feel and polish have not been judged against anything.
  • Stills cannot judge pacing, motion or audio. Every critic said so explicitly. The vignette runs about three minutes and was reviewed from five frames.

What changed this pass

Scene coverage 23/39 → 26/39. Vignette 7 and the thread it opens: death.dmt → dmt.hyperspace → dmt.sent-back. Two of the seven deaths are now built, and one of them is the one the player survives.

  • The thread is made of choices, not of watching. The first decision is live 9 seconds into the vignette; there are six across the three scenes, at 9s, 29s, 10s, 26s, 42s and 8s of their own scenes; and no stretch anywhere leaves the player without something to decide for longer than about fifteen seconds. Every one moves karma, harmony, will or attachment, every one has a stated default for a player who says nothing, and every scene’s exit is live from its first frame.
  • The characterisation is the first choice. The man is established by being asked what he holds on to — the pencil marks on the doorframe, the hall he never finished, or the window — rather than by a minute of watching him first. The three options are the three things actually modelled in his flat, the room lights whichever one he picks, and the pick is quoted back at him twice: once by the entities, who ask to see it, and once on his return, when he is handed the whole life back.
  • The spirit body answers a choice. Showing the entities what he brought moves karma by +1 and refusing moves it by −1, which crosses a band in systems/spirit-body.ts in either direction: measured at 0xc9bcd8/0.78 before, 0xffd9a8/0.95 after. The player can see it by looking down on the next frame. Franchezzo’s idea used rather than decorated (L-FRAN-03).
  • Decisions travel between scenes through soul.shards rather than through a new field invented for one vignette. Shards are already the run’s record of what it found, already surfaced by the test API, already carried into memories at the river. A replay of the vignette inside the same run clears the decision shards and deliberately keeps the memory ones, so a second pass cannot tell the player they are still holding something they put down a life ago.
  • The return is written down while he is alive. Everything else in this game persists by dying, and there is no river between this scene and the rest of his week, so recordUnlock writes the memory and a line of wisdom straight to the incarnation. Which line depends on what he decides to carry back, so the thread that is unlocked is shaped by the choice that unlocked it. Verified durable through the test API.
  • The raymarcher is built to a budget, not to a resolution. The fractal is marched into its own render target at a fraction of the canvas and shown on one full-screen quad, so its cost is a number the module controls rather than the size of the window. That fraction adapts to measured wall-clock frame time, starting low and climbing, and the step count and fold depth fall with it. There is one extra render per frame and no extra post-processing pass. On this container’s SwiftShader, dmt.hyperspace measures cheaper than either of the two room scenes beside it (p95 50ms against 66ms and 68ms).
  • Two real bugs in the raymarcher, found by looking at the frame buffer rather than at the code. First: the distance estimator was so fine-grained that almost every ray ran out of steps before reaching a surface, and the scene rendered as a soft gradient with no architecture in it — mean luma 20, standard deviation 9.6, not one bright pixel. It compiled, drew, counted frames and passed every check the gate had before this week. The fix was a hollow shell, fewer folds, and domain repetition so the structure is endless — a camera that drifts for two minutes can no longer leave the fractal behind and stare into empty space. Second: the proximity-glow and silhouette terms were sums over the steps taken, so the picture got brighter whenever the step budget rose. The scene would have brightened because the machine got faster, and flickered while the resolution controller settled. Both are now averages over the budget.
  • Brightness was budgeted deliberately, in both directions. Between those two states the scene overshot to mean luma 183 with 100% of sampled pixels bright, which passes the gate’s clipping bound and is still a glowing fog. Settled at mean 85, standard deviation 45, 47% bright, 0.00% clipped, with the bloom threshold raised to 0.86 so only the near faces of the architecture bloom.
  • The room he leaves and the room he returns to are the same function. buildFlat is called by both scenes with one control, fold, and the return runs it backwards. Two separately modelled rooms would have read as two rooms, and the whole weight of the scene is that it is demonstrably the same place, down to the line where the paint stops.
  • Content rule. death.dmt opens with the experience already beginning. No object in the room belongs to one, no action is depicted, and no substance, quantity, route or method is named in the scene, its captions, its content notes or its code. The lore bible’s § 6 note now records that as built, and § 12.5’s open question about where Franchezzo’s astral entities belong is answered consciously rather than by accident: the hyperspace entities come from L-DMT-03 alone and the two populations are deliberately not merged.

What changed in the previous pass

Scene coverage 12/39 → 21/39. Path A now runs life review → Council → seven Life Market aisles → checkout.

  • Pacing. The heart-attack vignette reached the afterlife at 3.5 minutes and then held its closing image indefinitely, so a player who simply watched could sit in a dead room well past that. It now runs 164s of beats plus a 10-second grace, after which the scene lets go by itself — 175s, under three minutes, with the exit live from the first frame for anyone who wants to leave sooner. That figure was only true at full frame rate. Trimming the beats fixed the script but not the clock: the timeline was advanced by the game loop’s clamped animation delta, so at roughly 3fps each 0.33s frame moved the story only 0.1s and the 165s vignette took over 500s to play. Pacing was a property of the machine, not of the game, which is why tuning beats could never fix it. The Director now advances to absolute wall-clock time since scene load, still bounded so a hidden tab does not skip beats nobody saw. The grace period had the identical bug — counting deltas to reach 10 seconds meant it took 27. 2:55, now on any renderer. Every beat was trimmed rather than any beat cut, so the arc keeps its shape. GAME_BRIEF.md was updated to match rather than left contradicting the build.
  • The Council. The guides weigh the heart against the feather, and the beam tilts by the karma the review recorded. Its real job is that it makes the ledger run end to end: the review writes karma, the Council reads it and locks obligations into the cart, and the Market honours those locks. Before this, karma was written and nothing read it.
  • The Life Market. Seven aisles and a checkout, sharing one mechanism rather than being seven hand-written scenes. 35 items, each carrying a sensory flash rather than a description. Locked obligations show as “already in your cart” and cannot be put back, which is what the brief specifies for unresolved karma.
  • The Market is a grocery aisle now, not shelves of light. The owner's note was that “the shopping aisle should look like an actual aisle in a grocery store”, against a reference photograph. The abstract plinths are gone. In their place (src/game/systems/grocery.ts): a run of shelving down both sides with uprights, five decks, a back panel and a pale price rail along every edge; a suspended ceiling of square tiles with recessed runs of light receding to the vanishing point; a polished speckled floor that returns those runs as streaks; a cross-aisle at the far end so the room is not a sealed box; and a cart standing in the aisle. All packaging is invented — abstract blocks, cans, bottles, jars and pouches in flat colour, with no mark or text on anything.
    It is built for SwiftShader, where the gate runs: one bay geometry instanced 36 times, five product archetypes instanced once each, three shader-drawn planes for the shell, and no lights at all — the overhead key is baked into vertex colours from the face normal. 1,171 product facings and 36 shelf bays land in 33 draw calls per frame (29 before, for the abstract version) at about 78,000 triangles, and the scene runs 14.3 ms mean / 52 ms p95 on software WebGL, against 19.1 ms / 66 ms for the plinths it replaced.
    Seven aisles still share one builder and differ only by light and copy. Trauma is lit the warmest room in the market, which is what the brief asks for: sacred, not grim.
  • The aisle panel was restyled for a bright scene. It was built for a dark one: prices and the cart line sat where the panel's fade had run out and were close to unreadable over a lit shelf. The fade now holds further, the dim greys were lifted, and the whole panel carries a text shadow. On a short phone viewport the nav is pinned to the foot of the panel — the longest aisle pushed it off the bottom edge, which is the exact shape of the four scenes that shipped stuck.
  • The seven “Life Market aisle through checkout” journeys now pass. They had skipped on every run since the first commit. This is the first time that part of the brief has been exercised at all.

Two economy decisions worth a second opinion

  • The numbers are a first guess. All pricing lives in one table in src/game/systems/market.ts, deliberately, because an economy spread across eight scene files cannot be tuned. Nobody has played it. With the slice's single review writing −1 karma, a soul arrives with one locked lesson and very little to spend — which may be tight, or may just be stingy. Only playing it will say.
  • The gift flashes are written to be tempting. The brief allows a cart of only gifts and has the guides warn that such a life teaches little. That warning is only worth anything if the gifted life is genuinely attractive; written as obviously hollow, the choice would be theatre and the warning redundant.

Known, named, not yet fixed

  • The reincarnation loop is still open. The River of Forgetting and rebirth are unbuilt, so the cart never becomes the next run's opening conditions — the one mechanic the game is named for. Checkout names them rather than pretending the path continues.
  • Six of seven vignettes are unbuilt, including the two carrying the brief's hardest content rules. Path B does not exist at all.
  • The kitchen has too few objects to read as lived-in, and its death still changes the lens more than the room — the vignette critic's biggest gap, only partly addressed.
  • Nothing in the Threshold has a horizon or an object of known size, so none of it conveys real scale.
  • The presences have no boundary, terminator or occlusion, so they read as light rather than as bodies.
  • Front-matter typography is still the browser's default UI stack.
  • No critic has reviewed the Council or the Market. Every verdict on this page predates both.
